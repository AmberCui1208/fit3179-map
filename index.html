Australian Fishery

Australia’s fisheries are vital to the nation’s economy, regional livelihoods, and food security. Yet, the scale and composition of fishery values differ widely across states and over time, making it hard to see regional patterns and long-term trends.

This dashboard visualises official data on fishery production across Australian states and territories, combining spatial and temporal views to reveal shifts in value and industry structure.

It is designed for Australians interested in seafood, regional economics, or sustainability — helping students, communities, and policymakers understand where and how the nation’s fisheries are changing.

Fishery Value: Trends & Distribution

Key Observations

  • Distribution: The map reveals clear regional disparities. Western Australia typically leads national value, while smaller jurisdictions jurisdictions contribute less.
  • Trend: State contributions fluctuate across 1998–2024, with a softening around 2010–2018 and a noticeable rebound after 2020.
  • NSW vs QLD: Queensland shows a marked uptick post-2020, while New South Wales is steadier overall—indicating shifting dominance within eastern fisheries.
  • Takeaway: Australia’s fishery value shows regional concentration but long-run resilience, balancing cyclical dips with recent recovery.

Context & Notes

Use the year selector on the map for spatial comparisons, and the brush on the stacked area chart to zoom into periods of change (e.g., post-2020 recovery).

TAC Overview & Variability

Australia uses Total Allowable Catch (TAC) — regulatory limits — to keep fisheries sustainable. Below are two key views to unpack TAC patterns:

  • Mosaic Chart (Top): Tiles show quota distribution across top species (tile size = TAC amount). It reveals which species dominate each year and how allocations shift over time.
  • Box Plot (Right): Summarises TAC variability per species — you’ll see which have stable quotas vs. large annual adjustments (median = typical quota; whiskers = min/max).

Why it matters

These visuals show how Australia balances ecological sustainability (via adjusted quotas) and economic yield (via consistent top-species allocations). Use the sliders to tweak the top species count and year range.

Commodity Mix & Change

Key Observations

  • Structure: The stream shows each commodity category’s share and level from 1989–2024.
  • Shifts: Cycles appear around the early 2000s and late 2010s.
  • Latest: Recent years show recovery in several categories; the mix remains concentrated.
  • Takeaway: Composition changes slowly; leaders explain total value trends.

Context & Notes

Use the legend to focus a category. The lollipop compares the selected year with a baseline for Top-N categories — position shows current value; colour shows increase or decrease.

Key Insights · Summary

  • Regional differences: WA/TAS often lead; NT/ACT remain lower.
  • Overall trend: Moderate growth with occasional dips and rebounds.
  • Structural dependence: A few high-value categories dominate.
  • Policy/market signals: Year-specific shifts may align with management or market changes.
